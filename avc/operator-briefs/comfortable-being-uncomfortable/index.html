Leadership note

The difficult conversation gives the work somewhere to go.

A slow month can fill a manager’s day with familiar work: another price discussion, another inventory walk, another meeting about the number. The follow-up conversation with an experienced salesperson is still waiting.

Experience earns trust. It also makes admitting a gap feel costly. A useful GM makes the gap specific, stays respectful, and gives the person a place to work on it. The next customer should benefit from what the team learns.

Worked hypothetical · Every event and figure is invented

Halfway through a hypothetical month

Assume one rooftop has 20 selling days and a target of 120 retail deliveries. After 10 selling days, it has delivered 48. An even pace would put it at 60, so it is 12 deliveries behind.

There are 72 deliveries left to reach the target, with 10 selling days remaining. That requires an average of 7.2 deliveries per day. The first half averaged 4.8. Those figures describe the pace the target requires; they do not predict what the store will sell.

The GM sits with the desk manager and reviews 10 unsold-visit records from this invented store. Six have no agreed next step. The sample gives them a specific conversation to work on. Traffic, available inventory and financing outcomes still need separate examination.

Suggested GM conversation

Show the record before judging the person

The desk manager has years of experience and usually remembers whom to call. The GM asks to see how an unsold visit ends. The aim is a clear agreement the next person can understand, including a customer’s decision to decline further contact.

A vague demand to show more urgency leaves the manager to guess what needs changing. The records let the GM ask for a concrete behavior and offer help practicing it.

We are 12 deliveries behind the halfway pace. In six of these 10 records, I cannot see what we agreed to do next.

Show me how you close an unsold visit. Let’s practice one clear next-step question together, then check a fresh record after your next conversation.

Suggested practice · Same hypothetical store

Give the next attempt a clear job

GM

Name the observable gap. Ask what made it difficult. Rehearse the conversation with the desk manager and make room for a correction to your own approach.

Desk manager

Work through one unsold-visit example with the salesperson. Give one usable correction, then ask for another attempt while the feedback is fresh.

Sales floor

Ask what the customer wants to do next. Record the agreed action and time accurately. Record a declined next step plainly; a filled field should reflect a real conversation.

Across rooftops

Share a clear example another store can practice. Let its GM explain the local obstacle before importing the same response. Keep each store’s pace and conditions visible.

When the work changes

A process change can expose a learning gap

Suppose the same store moves its follow-up work into one shared list. An experienced manager may know the customers well and still need help using the new fields. Ask where the process becomes unclear. Walk through a practice record together before expecting someone to use it independently.

The GM has work to do here too. If the field is confusing or the handoff adds an unnecessary step, hear the explanation before defending the rollout. A clear standard and a workable process make the next attempt more useful.

Give a newer employee a smaller case and more help. Give an experienced employee a less familiar customer response to work through. Keep rehearsal out of the customer’s live negotiation, and keep correction respectful. Public embarrassment makes confusion harder to surface.

Suggested observations · Results still need measuring

Check what carries into the real work

Conversation

Can the person make the ask clearly?

After a rehearsal, check a fresh unsold-visit record and ask the salesperson to explain what the customer agreed to.

Result

Keep the delivery pace visible.

Report actual deliveries against the store’s target. A clearer record is one improvement to examine; the volume result has other inputs.

Next desk review

Come back after the rehearsal

At the next desk review, look at a new visit handled with less prompting. If the agreement is still unclear, ask where the conversation broke down and adjust the practice. One polished rehearsal can leave the real difficulty untouched.

Bring one useful example and one unresolved obstacle to the next GM conversation across rooftops. Let the team see that an experienced leader can accept a correction, make another attempt, and keep the operating standard in place.
