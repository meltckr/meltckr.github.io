Comfortable being uncomfortable starts with the conversation a slow month makes easy to postpone.

In this worked dealership example, every number is invented. A store has twenty selling days and a target of one hundred twenty deliveries. After ten days, it has delivered forty eight. That is twelve behind an even pace. Seventy two deliveries remain, requiring seven point two a day over the last ten days. The first half averaged four point eight.

The general manager and desk manager review ten unsold-visit records. Six have no agreed next step. That small review gives them a place to practice. Traffic, inventory and financing still need their own examination.

The general manager can say, we are twelve behind the pace. In six of these ten records, I cannot see what we agreed to do next. Show me how you close an unsold visit. I will practice the conversation with you.

The desk manager rehearses one clear question, gets a correction, and tries again. The salesperson records the action and time the customer actually agrees to. If the customer declines, the record says so.

When a new follow-up process is confusing, the manager needs to hear that too. Choose a small practice case, explain the field, and let the person make another attempt.

Later, check a fresh visit with less help. Look for a clearer conversation and an accurate record. Keep reporting the delivery pace separately.

An experienced team can keep its standards and make room to learn. Start with one conversation you can improve, then see what changes on the floor.
