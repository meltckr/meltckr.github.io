This is a hypothetical dealership deal. Every number and event is invented for this example.

At ten minutes past seven on Friday evening, finance and insurance receives a worksheet showing eighteen thousand dollars for a trade. The desk appraised the trade at seventeen thousand dollars. The salesperson has already quoted eighteen thousand dollars. Under this example's store rule, the desk can approve seventeen thousand five hundred dollars. The customer's quoted figure needs the general manager's approval.

The general manager is away. The desk asks for help with a trade, but leaves out the figures. The answer arrives eighteen minutes later. The revised worksheet takes another six minutes. The customer's hold lasts twenty-four minutes.

Assume every other deal figure stays fixed. The extra one thousand dollars for the trade comes out of the expected gross profit. The customer's waiting time has no dollar cost assigned in this example.

Before Monday opening, write the approval rule and name a backup for the general manager. The same desk manager follows the request until finance accepts the approved worksheet.

Use a message the manager can answer. The trade appraisal is seventeen thousand dollars. The customer was quoted eighteen thousand dollars. My limit is seventeen thousand five hundred dollars. Please approve the customer's quoted figure or return a figure I can give them. Finance is waiting. Please answer within five minutes.

Five minutes is a trial target. Measure actual reply times. Each over-limit worksheet should carry its written approval. Count worksheets that finance sends back. Count each deal once, and record why the worksheet came back.

On Monday, review the first week's reply times with the desk manager. Open one packet finance sent back and fix its missing field before the next customer arrives.
